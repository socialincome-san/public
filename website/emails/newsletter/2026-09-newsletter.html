Want to open a Country Office? Journal

Is it your bday today?

Chances are 1 in 365. (Nerds who say "well, not exactly" are right, but go here to argue). But here's a number we can all agree on: the chance you have a birthday coming up is a flat 100%. And now there's something worth doing about it — you can set up a birthday campaign to collect donations from friends and family, with 100% of it going straight to the program of your choice.

No more scented candles and books that go straight to the shelf.

Teenager vibes

Remember that weird waiting room between kid and adult? Too young to be taken seriously all the time. Too old to get away with anything. That's a bit how it feels at Social Income right now. In plenty of areas, we've gotten seriously good at what we do — and yet we're still a bunch of volunteers, no overhead, running the whole thing on free time and a lot of heart. Next week we're all meeting up in a library on top of a mountain to sit with some of the tough questions:

How much do we want to grow? How solid is our volunteer model? And how do we stay the best NGO north of the South Pole?

New kid

If cash is one way to unlock potential, AI might be another. A new randomized trial in Sierra Leone suggests so. In eight weeks, students using Google's AI-powered "Guided Learning" made 1.2 to 1.7 years of progress in maths, not because AI handed them the answers, but because it helped them think through the problems.

Looks like AI finally found an audience that actually does its homework, if used intelligently.

Playground bullies

While tech bros flex their neurons on ever-bigger models, African innovators are taking a different path: smaller, leaner AI built for local languages, patchy connectivity and everyday needs. The catch? Most of the data behind today's biggest models is heavily English-focused, leaving many African languages, and the people who speak them, underrepresented.

The current gameplay for AI globally is completely un-African.

My World in Data

40%

By 2030, 40% of the world's young people will be African. By 2050, the continent will drive 85% of the growth in the world's working-age population.

Some people find that scary. There is even a word for it: Afrophobia. We see it the other way around. It will be the biggest pool of talent and ideas the world has ever had. All it needs is a chance. And the surest way to give it is to lift the one thing holding it back: poverty.

1.3 billion

That's how many people worldwide still have no bank account, or any kind of money account at all. But things are moving. In Sub-Saharan Africa, 40% of adults now have one, up from 27% in 2021. That is real progress. would still be considered poor.

And who pulled it off? Banks? Nope. Mobile providers? Bingo.
