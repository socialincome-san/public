Mango season

There are more than 1,000 varieties of mangoes in the world. At least according to an article I stumbled across while looking for something completely different.

But mangoes also illustrate something more interesting: agricultural products can travel through surprisingly long supply chains before reaching us.

A farmer might receive only a tiny fraction of what we eventually pay at the supermarket.

Follow one mango’s journey

Next time I’m standing in front of the fruit shelf, I’ll probably think about this. For approximately five seconds.

The bus experiment

Imagine receiving $50 every month simply for taking public transport instead of driving.

That’s roughly what one city is testing as part of a new experiment to reduce traffic. Early results suggest people are surprisingly willing to change their habits when incentives are simple.

Whether the experiment survives beyond its pilot phase is another question.

Read about the experiment

Money without banks

More than a billion adults worldwide still don’t have a traditional bank account.

But increasingly, that doesn’t necessarily mean they can’t send or receive money.

Mobile wallets, digital currencies and new payment infrastructure are changing what it means to be “banked” in the first place.

Sometimes infrastructure skips a generation.

31%

A new study found that households receiving regular cash payments increased their savings by 31% compared with the control group.

Interestingly, researchers also found changes in how families planned larger expenses.

Cash changes today. Predictability can change tomorrow.
